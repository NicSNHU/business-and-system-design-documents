Features a Business Requirements document and a System Design Document for client DriverPass as demonstrated through course CS 255


** Project Summary **

The client was DriverPass, a company owned by Liam that helps students prepare for DMV driving tests. DriverPass wanted a cloud-based web system that would allow students to register online, choose from flexible training packages, schedule and manage on-the-road driving lessons, take online classes and practice tests, and track their progress. Staff needed the tools to manage reservations, packages, user accounts, driver notes, and activity reports, while also receiving updates from the DMV so that the training materials stay current.

** What I did particularly well ** 

I did particularly well creating UML diagrams (Use Case, Activity, Sequence, Class) with CASE tools that accurately capture the system's roles, flows, and data relationships. The Use Case Diagram showed the different Actors (Customer, Secretary, Driver/Instructor, Owner, IT Supervisor, DMV) and how they interact with the system. The class diagram effectively models the core entities (Customer, Package, Reservation, Driver, Vehicle, TestProgress, etc.) and their relationships. 

** What I would revise ** 

If I could revise one part, I would expand the nonfunctional requirements sections with more measurable targets such as exact concurrent user capacity and response-time goals. I would also add a section discussing alternative design approaches so reasoning behind final decisions is transparent. 

** Interpreting and Implementing **

I interpreted the client's needs by carefully reviewing the interview information and breaking high-level goals (such as better student preparation, easy scheduling, and progress tracking to name a few) into distinct Functional and Non-Functional requirements. These were then translated into UML diagrams and system components. Considering the user's needs is essential because a design that looks good on paper but does not solve the real world problem that the students and staff have is not successful. Each major feature implemented derives from a stated need from DriverPass. 

** Design approach and future strategies ** 

I approached the design by first gathering and clarifying requirements. Then, I created UML diagrams to model actors, processes, and data as well as finally defining the system architecture, roles, and non-functional constraints. In the future I would continue to use this structured process and place even more emphasis on early validation with stakeholders, quantifiable non-functional requirements, and documenting the alternatives and tradeoffs.
